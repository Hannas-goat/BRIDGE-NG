Before the Interview

Get ready with confidence
  • Research the company thoroughly.
  • Learn the company's mission and values.
  • Read the job description several times.
  • Match your skills to the job requirements.
  • Research the interviewer if their name is available.
  • Practice answering common interview questions.
  • Prepare your own questions for the interviewer.
  • Print extra copies of your resume.
  • Bring a notebook and pen.
  • Know where the interview location is.
  • Do a practice drive or check transportation.
  • Plan to arrive 10–15 minutes early.
  • Choose professional clothing the day before.
  • Make sure your clothes are clean and wrinkle-free.
  • Get a good night's sleep.
  • Eat a light meal before the interview.
  • Turn your phone completely off or to silent.
  • Review your accomplishments.
  • Think of examples that show leadership.
  • Think of examples that show teamwork.

Making a Great First Impression

First few minutes matter
  • Smile when you meet people.
  • Offer a confident handshake (if appropriate).
  • Make eye contact.
  • Introduce yourself clearly.
  • Be polite to everyone — including receptionists.
  • Show enthusiasm.
  • Sit with good posture.
  • Avoid slouching.
  • Don't interrupt the interviewer.
  • Listen carefully before answering.

Answering Questions

Let your experience speak for itself
  • Speak clearly.
  • Don't rush your answers.
  • Be honest.
  • Use specific examples.
  • Tell stories instead of giving one-word answers.
  • Use the STAR Method (Situation, Task, Action, Result).
  • Focus on what you learned.
  • Highlight transferable skills.
  • Explain school projects confidently.
  • Mention volunteer experience.
  • Talk about internships.
  • Discuss extracurricular activities.
  • Explain leadership roles.
  • Don't criticize previous employers or teachers.
  • Admit when you don't know something.
  • Stay positive.
  • Show willingness to learn.
  • Explain how you solve problems.
  • Mention times you overcame challenges.
  • Demonstrate confidence without arrogance.

Body Language

Your posture speaks before you do
  • Keep good eye contact.
  • Smile naturally.
  • Avoid crossing your arms.
  • Don't fidget.
  • Keep your hands relaxed.
  • Nod while listening.
  • Sit up straight.
  • Avoid looking at your phone.
  • Keep your feet still.
  • Show energy through your posture.

Questions You Should Be Ready For

Common — and totally answerable
  • Tell me about yourself.
  • Why do you want this job?
  • Why should we hire you?
  • What are your strengths?
  • What is one weakness you're working on?
  • Tell me about a challenge you've faced.
  • Describe a time you worked on a team.
  • Tell me about a time you showed leadership.
  • Where do you see yourself in five years?
  • Why did you choose your major?
  • Tell me about a project you're proud of.
  • How do you handle stress?
  • Describe a difficult situation.
  • What motivates you?
  • Why do you want to work here?

Questions You Should Ask the Employer

Interviews go both ways
  • What does success look like in this role?
  • What training is provided?
  • What does a typical day look like?
  • What qualities make someone successful here?
  • What opportunities are there for growth?
  • How is performance evaluated?
  • What is the team culture like?
  • What are the biggest challenges in this role?
  • What do you enjoy most about working here?
  • What are the next steps in the hiring process?

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Easy to sidestep once you know them
  • Arriving late.
  • Dressing too casually.
  • Speaking negatively about others.
  • Giving overly long answers.
  • Using slang or inappropriate language.
  • Forgetting the interviewer's name.
  • Not asking any questions.
  • Lying on your resume.
  • Looking bored or distracted.
  • Forgetting to thank the interviewer.

After the Interview

Finish as strong as you started
  • Thank the interviewer before leaving.
  • Send a thank-you email within 24 hours.
  • Reflect on what went well.
  • Learn from any mistakes.
  • Stay confident — every interview is valuable experience, whether you get the job or not.
